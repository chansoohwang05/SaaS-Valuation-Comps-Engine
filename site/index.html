Forty

In 2021 a point of growth was worth 2.4 points of margin. Across the past two years, 0.8. The Rule of 40 calls them equal.

Every quarter since 2019, a cross-sectional regression of US software companies on their own filed fundamentals, asking what the market paid for a point of growth and what it paid for a point of free cash flow margin. Averaged over seven years the two come out close to equal — which is the Rule of 40's assumption, and which conceals a complete reversal between the zero-rate era and now.

What this is not

A residual is not a valuation. A company trading above the line may be growing into it, may have a mix the model cannot see, or may simply be expensive; the model has three variables and the market has more. Free cash flow margin is measured after stock-based compensation is added back in operating cash flow, which flatters software companies specifically — the number is standard, and standard is not the same as right. Coefficients are estimated on one quarter of data each, so a single quarter's estimate is noisy; the count of quarters rejecting equal weighting matters more than any one of them. Nothing here is investment advice.
